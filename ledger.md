# Decision Ledger

A before/during record for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon build (Oct 26 to Nov 4, 2026).

## Rules of this ledger

- Every material decision gets a dated entry: what was decided, why, and what it rules out.
- "Before" entries describe pre-event background (pilot, labeled data, operator experience). They are never presented as hackathon-built work.
- "During" entries describe work built inside the event window only. The demo highlights during-entries only.

- 2026-10-27 (during): First eval ablation on the 89-message set (6 few-shot examples, n=83 scored per arm). spark_only: 73.5% overall, 76.6% decisive, Act recall 50% (errors skew to Confirm). wonder_only: best answered-row quality (88.5% decisive, 88.6% high-confidence) but 27 of 83 calls timed out at the client's 20 s timeout and fell back to fail-safe Confirm, so its 62.7% overall measures latency, not judgment. escalate: 68.7% overall; only 4 of 83 messages escalated because Spark is overconfident (70 of 83 confidences at 0.85+, high-confidence accuracy no better than overall). Total spend $3.08. Fix chosen with the operator: raise request_timeout_secs 20 -> 75 and re-run the Wonder and escalate arms before any prompt tuning. Scorecards live in eval/runs/ (uncommitted, under review).
- 2026-10-27 (during): Timeout rerun at 75 s, sequential (a first parallel attempt was contaminated: running both arms against one account produced fast transport errors the client reports identically to timeouts, 29 and 48 fail-safe rows; those two run dirs are not results). wonder_only at 75 s: zero timeouts, overall 73.5%, decisive 81.4% (n=43), high-confidence 86.0%, Act recall 46.4% (13/28), $2.82. escalate at 75 s: zero fail-safes, all 5 escalations completed (3 correct), overall 69.9%, decisive 72.9%, $1.07, cheapest per correct decisive call ($0.030 vs Wonder-only $0.081). Conclusion: latency is solved; the remaining Act weakness is judgment in the shared prompt, not the models. Both tiers downgrade roughly half of true-Act messages to Confirm. Next lever is prompt tuning for urgent-message recall, then a Spark-first re-test. No arm yet meets the ladder's P2 thresholds, so Jev correctly stays at approve-first.
- 2026-10-27 (during): Prompt tune landed and committed (e1a2c9d). Diagnosis: the original prompt defined Confirm to include change requests and refunds and ordered "when in doubt choose Confirm", so both models parked urgent items there. The rewrite adds hard Act rules (lockout/code failure, water/leak, heat/AC, safety, broken-in-home, booking changes with dates or money, same/next-day arrival questions), an access/water/heat/safety tie-breaker, and confidence discipline (0.85+ only for clear-cut cases). Results (n=83): spark Act recall 50.0% -> 75.0%; escalate Act recall 42.9% -> 82.1% and escalate+tuned is now the best overall arm at 74.7% with Ignore at 100% and 12 of 12 escalations completed. The cost: Confirm accuracy fell to 48.4% in both tuned arms (roughly half of true-Confirm messages now over-called Act, the cheap error direction at P1, but it drags decisive numbers). Next iteration target, if run: sharpen the Confirm boundary from the other side without giving back the Act rules. Cumulative eval spend ~$11.46 of $150.

Engine + early check-in committed with tests; suite at 26 passing. Turno access is a fixture adapter behind a clean interface (real API is read-only); no real messages sent, no real charges.
- 2026-10-27 (during): Second prompt pass (tuned2) is the configuration of record; production policy = escalate. Diagnosis from the tuned runs' false Act calls: pre-booking price haggling, money talk with no live change, what-the-home-provides questions, and checkout-procedure questions were being swept into Act. Fix: symmetric Confirm hard rules (price talk on an unbooked stay is Confirm; an inquiry still being arranged is Confirm; provision/how-things-work questions and checkout steps are Confirm; a complaint or vent with no request and nothing broken is Confirm) plus an explicit decision order (Act hard rules first; otherwise Confirm if a human should weigh in, Ignore if nothing is needed). Results (n=83): escalate tuned2 overall 84.3% (best of the ablation), decisive 81.8%, high-confidence 88.5%, Confirm 80.6%, Act recall 75.0%, Ignore 100%, Confirm->Act errors zero in both arms. Spark tuned2: overall 83.1%, Act recall 78.6% (its best). Residual: ~6 Confirm rows per arm now fall to Ignore, the safest error direction; noted as the target if a third pass is ever run. Cumulative eval spend ~$13.59 of $150.
