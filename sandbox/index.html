ComfıAutopılot

An autonomous operations system for short-term rentals, designed to connect to Hospitable; this build runs on a staged fixture world (no live Hospitable or Turno connection). Start with the first step of the operation: paste a guest message. Triage returns a category, a confidence, a draft in the operator's voice, a route, and a reason, plus the send right that category has earned so far. The urgency decision is made by the triage layer in this repo, a hackathon-built port of the operator's Jev pipeline (TypeSafe AI) to Muse Spark/Wonder; Jev's original tool, the hand-labeled dataset, and the Oct 7–14 pilot are pre-existing.

Staged demo world. The properties, turnovers, cleaners, and arrivals below are fixture data — no real guest data anywhere. Every send in a play is simulated or held for approval: nothing real is sent and nothing real is charged. Action steps show the gate the autonomy ladder applies before anything may execute.

Play: early check-in

A guest asks to arrive early. Comfi Autopilot extracts the requested time, checks the staged calendar (is there a same-day turnover? who cleans? when is the unit actually ready?), drafts the reply, and gates the early check-in charge — the highest-stakes action — for a human. When the answer depends on the cleaner, the run waits, and the cleaner's report finishes the chain.

Play: cleaner done → guest ready

No guest message at all: the cleaner finishing is the trigger. Comfi Autopilot verifies the clean against the record, then the arriving guest hears the place is ready. If the cleaner flagged a problem, the guest hears nothing and a human takes over.

Run timeline

Play: in-stay issue → handyman dispatch

A guest who is already in the home reports something broken. Comfi Autopilot identifies the issue from the message, checks who is actually staying, drafts the reply in the operator's voice, and composes the handyman dispatch — the dispatch send is gated in the middle of the ladder. A safety issue (gas, smoke, flooding, no heat) skips the fix-it advice entirely, gives the guest safety guidance, and flags the operator in the same run. When the vendor sends an ETA, and later reports the work done, the guest hears about each step — every send gated. The vendor and his rates are fixtures; the dispatch is simulated or held.

Play: special request → late checkout

A departing guest asks to check out late. Comfi Autopilot reads the staged calendar for that property and day — is a new guest arriving after them, how long is the turnover in between, what does the house allow — and drafts the answer in the operator's voice: yes for the time asked, a no that offers the latest time that does work when the ask runs into the next guest's turnover, or a question back when no time was given. The first hour past checkout is free; past that, the late checkout fee is its own step at the highest-stakes gate, held for a human, and only for a time the guest actually asked for. Same message, same day, two calendars below. Nothing is sent and nothing is charged.

Cameras

Play: camera check

An outside camera reports motion — no message from anyone. Comfi Autopilot classifies the staged frame, checks it against the staged calendar, and either logs it (the guest who was expected, the cleaner who was scheduled, nothing of note) or flags the operator (a crowd bigger than the booking, a person nobody expected, a frame too dark to judge). The motion play never messages a guest. Two Tier-1 extensions ride the same rails: a camera that goes offline during a stay, on a switch the guest controls, drafts the camera system's one guest message — held at the gate, in the operator's chosen wording — and a house marked closed flips the expectation, so any person flags the operator with the frame still attached. A post-turnover check reads the door and lights state of the emptied home. The final-list batch rides the same rails: a covered or turned camera flags the operator whatever the scene (urgent on a one-night weekend stay), a person stuck at a shut door across two frames flags the operator with the door state, a lockout report hands the operator the latest door frame, a guest who feels unsafe gets the latest frames to the operator urgently, and a sunset check reads the entry lights before an arriving guest does. Batch 2 rides the same rails: a dog arriving with the party when the booking lists no pet flags the operator with the still (nothing is charged; the call is the operator's), a morning check reads the snow at an empty home, cameras dropping together across properties collapse into one area-outage notification, and a periodic upkeep check turns the fleet's own history into turnover notes (check the WiFi, wipe the lens). Batch 3 rides the same rails: a gathering no longer fires on one frame — the people estimates accumulate across the stay, and the operator hears only when a sustained floor clears the booked party by a wide margin, worded as “at least N people (model estimate)”; a vendor's stated arrival window gets proof of service — a worker on camera inside the window is logged with the frame, and a window that closes with nobody seen goes to the operator, presence only and never a word about the work; and boxes carried in on three distinct days of one stay raise one awareness-only note: signs consistent with a possible move-in. The frames are staged metadata, not real photos.
